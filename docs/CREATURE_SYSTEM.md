# Creature System

## Principle

Every creature should have a useful role in the player's world. Species rarity alone is not a progression system. The definitions test enforces that every species can work at least one building and every production building has a capable species.

## Species definitions

| ID | Name | Element | Primary work | Affinities | Capture chance | Spawn weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `creature_rockhorn` | Rockhorn | Earth | Mining | mining 1.25 | 60% | 3 |
| `creature_embercub` | Embercub | Fire | Smelting | smelting 1.25 | 45% | 2 |
| `creature_mossling` | Mossling | Nature | Farming | farming 1.25 | 70% | 4 |
| `creature_voltfox` | Voltfox | Electric | Power | power 1.25 | 35% | 1 |
| `creature_timberpaw` | Timberpaw | Nature | Logging | logging 1.25 | 70% | 4 |
| `creature_aquafin` | Aquafin | Water | Water work | water 1.25, farming 1.1 | 55% | 3 |

No building uses water work yet, so Aquafin irrigates Farms at a reduced 1.1 affinity until water structures are designed.

## Owned instances

Persisted fields: unique instance ID, species ID, level, experience, trait IDs, optional mutation ID, optional assigned building ID.

New instances begin at level 1 with zero experience and no traits. IDs are generated by the server (`c_<guid>`) and must be unique inside the profile. A player may own several instances of one species.

Genetics are deliberately absent from schema version 1.

## Starter creature

On the first successful profile load the server grants one `creature_rockhorn` and records `starter_creature_granted = true`. The marker is written in the same transaction as the creature, so the grant happens exactly once.

## Capture

Wild creatures spawn at fixed points in the Wilds, species chosen by spawn weight, and wander near their spawn point. Capturing uses a server-owned ProximityPrompt. The server:

1. confirms the creature is still there (first successful capture wins),
2. checks the player's character is within range,
3. applies a 2-second per-player capture cooldown,
4. checks habitat capacity,
5. rolls the species capture chance,
6. on success runs `CreatureOwnershipDomain.capture` through the transactional pipeline and despawns the creature; it respawns 20–40 seconds later.

A failed roll leaves the creature in place.

## Capacity and release

Players can own **4 + 4 per Creature Habitat level** creatures. Capture fails with `creature_capacity_full` at the limit.

`ReleaseCreature` removes an idle creature permanently. Assigned creatures must be unassigned first, and the last owned creature cannot be released.

## Presentation

Assigned workers stand beside their building. Idle creatures stand in a pen just outside the north edge of their owner's plot, laid out by `WorldLayout.penSlotXZ`; the pen holds the largest possible roster (64 creatures) without reaching the next plot row. All of this is rendered from profile data and never read back.

## Levels

Workers gain one experience point per production cycle. Reaching the next level costs `50 × current level` experience; the maximum level is 10. Each level above 1 adds 5% to that worker's contribution.

## Work assignment

`WorkAssignmentDomain.validate` checks eligibility:

- the profile owns the creature and the building, and both point to canonical definitions,
- the creature is not assigned elsewhere,
- the building's worker list is dense, unique and consistent with each worker's own assignment,
- the building has free canonical worker capacity and a work type,
- the species has a positive canonical affinity for that work type.

`WorkAssignmentDomain.assign` settles the building's production at its old rate, then writes `creature.assignedBuildingId` and appends to `building.assignedCreatureIds` together. `unassign` settles and removes both sides; a dangling assignment to a missing building is simply cleared.

On every load `ProfileIntegrity.repair` removes one-sided, duplicate, incompatible or over-capacity assignment references. It only removes references, never grants anything, and logs how many it fixed.

## Future systems

Breeding, inherited genetics, mutations, combat builds, trading value, and creature marketplace behaviour are later specifications. They must extend the ownership model without changing stable species identity.
